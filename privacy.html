Household Reminders

Privacy Policy

Effective November 14, 2026

Household Reminders is a private app used only by the members of one household. This policy explains what it accesses and what it does with it.

What the app accesses

  • Google Tasks: the tasks in the "Household" list the app creates in a member's Google Tasks: titles, notes, due dates and whether they are completed. The app does not use any other task lists.
  • Email address: the Google account's email address, only to confirm that the right household member signed in.
  • Gmail (only if a member connects it): read-only access to that member's new emails, to suggest tasks, events, packages, notices and deals. The app can't send, delete or change anything. Emails likely to matter are sent to Claude to be understood; suggestions are shown only to that member, and only what they approve is kept. Security codes and similar messages are skipped.
  • Email: messages household members send or forward to the app's own address (for example a bill or appointment notice), to update the shared list, including the text of attached PDFs (read in memory on the household's computer, not stored). From forwarded emails the app keeps a short summary, the important links, the original sender and subject, and shipping tracking numbers. Full account and card numbers are not stored.

How it is used and stored

The dashboard is reached through Cloudflare, which carries the encrypted connection to the household's computer.

This information is used only to keep the household's shared to-do list and each member's "Household" task list in sync. It is stored on the household's own computer. Sign-in tokens are kept in a private file on that computer.

The app does not sell, share or transfer this information to anyone, does not show ads, and does not use it for any other purpose. Free text from emails may be processed by an AI model (Anthropic's Claude, through the household's Claude subscription or, when that is busy, Anthropic's API under the same terms) solely to turn a member's message into list changes, a member's book titles, star ratings, short reviews and current reading are sent to Claude solely to suggest books for that member, and the library's book titles and authors are sent to Claude to sort them into categories. A photo of a book's cover or of a shelf, when used to add books, is sent to Claude only to read the titles and authors, and deleted right after. To pick the day's top news, public news headlines from news feeds (and which stories members marked interesting or not) are sent to Claude; nothing else about the household is included.

Household Reminders' use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Household calendar

The app keeps a shared "Household" calendar in its own Google account (not in members' accounts). It can only manage calendars it created and who they're shared with. It reads that calendar's upcoming events to show them in the morning email and on the dashboard.

Library

To show book details and covers, the app looks books up on Open Library by ISBN or title (no personal information is sent), and the dashboard loads cover images from Open Library and the Internet Archive. When Open Library is missing a cover or description, the ISBN may also be looked up on Google Books, and choosing a new cover searches Apple Books by title and author. A cover photo you upload is stored with the household's data and shown only to signed-in members.

Scanning a barcode uses your phone's camera only while the scanner is open. The barcode is read on your phone; no video or photo leaves it, only the ISBN it finds.

Weather

To show the weather, the app sends the household's approximate location (city-level coordinates, no names or accounts) to Open-Meteo, a free weather service.

Drive to work

If you enter a work address, it and the household's home address are turned into map coordinates once, when saved, by OpenStreetMap's free address search. On work days, the morning job sends only those two sets of coordinates and your usual leaving time to TomTom to estimate the drive and find incidents on the route; no names, accounts or other household data. Only you see your work address, and you can remove it in Settings at any time.

Phone notifications

If you turn on notifications on a device, the app stores that device's notification address and sends short alerts (a task's title, a highlight's title, a badge, a bill that's due tomorrow (its name and amount)) through the phone or browser maker's own push service (Apple, Google, Mozilla or Microsoft), encrypted so that the service can't read them. Turning them off, signing out on that device, or leaving the household deletes it. A nudge from another member is one of these alerts: it is sent only if you have nudges turned on and you're not in your quiet hours, and the household sees that it was sent.

Gym

Each person's training plan (the week, weights and reps logged, lift history, records, past weeks, body weight entered and measurements) is stored with the household's data and visible to everyone in the household; only its owner can change it. Progress photos are stored the same way but shown only to their owner, unless the owner turns on sharing in Settings. Deleting a photo removes it. The Monday recap email and the timeline show each person's sessions done, streak and which lifts got a new record, never weights, measurements or photos.

Shopping list, groceries and what's new

Things added to the shopping list (title, who it's for, a rough price, a product link, notes and what it cost) are shared with everyone in the household and stored with the household's list. The store links are plain searches built from the title; the app never visits the stores or fetches prices. The grocery list (each item, its aisle, a short note, who added it and who ticked it off) is shared with everyone in the household too; finished trips (what was bought and who finished the trip) are kept for about four months, to show past trips and suggest the things you buy often (and when they're probably running low), then deleted. If you choose Not now or Stop suggesting on a suggestion, that choice is kept with the list. To show what's new since your last visit, the app stores the time you last had it open. Changes made in the app (a task done, a grocery trip, a light turned off) are shown on the household's timeline with who made them, and kept with the household's data.

Bills

Bills the household tracks (who the bill is from, the day it is usually due, how often it comes, whether it pays itself, who it is for, who added it, an amount when a member enters one, and when each was paid and by whom) are stored with the household's list and seen by everyone in the household. If you choose Not a bill on a suggested bill, that choice is kept with the list. Household only keeps track of bills: it never pays anything, never signs in to a bank or a biller, and never stores account or card numbers.

Smart home and the Frame TV

The app shows and controls only the home devices the household has chosen to share with it (lights, plugs, the thermostat, speakers, sensors), through the household's own Home Assistant, which runs on the same computer at home. Device names, rooms and states are read when the page is open and not stored; each change made from the app is recorded on the timeline. To tell whether anyone is home, the app checks Home Assistant's at-home status for the household's people at that moment; locations are not read or kept.

When a speaker is playing, the app fetches the album art for it from Home Assistant (or from the music service's own picture address) and shows it to signed-in members; it is kept only briefly in memory, not stored.

Photos added to the Frame TV are sent from your phone to the household's computer and on to the TV over the home network; the copy on the computer is deleted once the TV has it. Small previews of the photos on the TV are kept on the household's computer so the page loads quickly, and are shown only to signed-in members. Removing a photo from the TV deletes its preview.

Uptime check

An outside monitoring service (UptimeRobot) checks every few minutes that the app is answering. It only receives a yes or no and the app's version, never any household information.

Removing access

A member can disconnect the app at any time at myaccount.google.com/permissions. Disconnecting stops the sync; the member's "Household" task list stays in their Google account until they delete it.
